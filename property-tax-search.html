⚠️ Portal not loading or link looks stale?

Local government record systems change frequently. If the destination does not open:

  • Start from the local government site. Look for Assessor, Property Appraiser, Auditor, PVA, Tax Commissioner, or Treasurer.
  • Allow required browser features. Some record systems need JavaScript, cookies, pop-ups, or an acknowledgement screen before search.
  • Use the alternative links above. NETR Online or Google can help locate a moved portal, but verify the destination belongs to the government office or a vendor linked by it.
  • Do not rely on an unfamiliar payment page. Confirm the domain from the local government website before entering payment or sensitive information.
  • Report stale links. Tell us about a broken portal so the directory can be corrected.
Pro tip: If the portal asks for a parcel number, you can usually find it by first searching the address; the parcel/PIN will appear on the result page.
How this tool works

Property records are administered differently across the U.S. — by counties, parishes, boroughs, cities, towns, districts, and in some cases statewide systems. This tool is a directory that routes you toward the relevant local record source.

STEP 01
Pick the state
All 50 states plus DC are covered. Some states, including Connecticut and Massachusetts, administer property assessment at the municipal level; Alaska also uses boroughs and municipalities. The tool now labels those areas accordingly.
STEP 02
Pick the jurisdiction
321 listed areas are mapped to direct record destinations or search fallbacks. If an area is not listed, the fallback options help you locate the correct assessor or tax office.
STEP 03
Search the records
Search capabilities vary by jurisdiction and vendor. The directory records whether address, parcel/PIN, or owner-name search is expected to be available, but the destination portal controls the actual fields and access rules.
Why we don't pull data ourselves

Property assessment and tax administration are decentralized, and local offices use many different government and vendor record systems. A single directory therefore needs jurisdiction-specific links and fallbacks.

We maintain a curated directory of government pages and locally linked record portals. TaxPreparerTools does not copy the property-record database into this page; you review records at the destination source.

Privacy note: property owner, parcel, and address searches happen after you leave this tool. If you use the ZIP shortcut, the ZIP is sent from your browser to Zippopotam and the returned coordinates are sent to the FCC area API to estimate the jurisdiction. You can always select the state and area manually instead.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a national property tax database?

There is no single federal property-tax record portal covering every U.S. jurisdiction. Records are generally administered by local governments, and the responsible level can be a county, parish, borough, municipality, city, town, district, or state system.

Do I have to pay or sign up to look up property tax records?

TaxPreparerTools is free to use. Most local lookup pages are also free to search, but a jurisdiction or records vendor may charge for copies, certified documents, payments, or other services.

What if my jurisdiction isn’t listed?

Use the Google fallback or NETR Online directory shown after selecting a state. These can help locate the correct local office, but verify the result is a government site or a records vendor linked by that government.

Why won’t a property portal load?

Local record systems can move, require JavaScript or cookies, undergo maintenance, or block some network traffic. Try the local government assessor or tax-office homepage, then the alternative directory/search links, and report stale URLs.
